## 0. The backdrop that drove both layers this month
- **Rates shock.** On Sept 16 the Fed raised rates 25 bp to 3.75–4.00%, its first hike since 2023 (AFP via Kuwait Times). August CPI was 3.4%. On Sept 24 the 10-yr hit 5.196%, the highest since 2007, and the 30-yr hit 5.48% (Reuters via Investing.com) [P]. In September the 10-yr rose 54 bp to 5.29%, its largest monthly rise in four years (Nasdaq, Oct 1) [S]. **Why it matters:** both layers are capital-intensive and increasingly debt-funded.
- **Oil shock.** The Iran war (US/Israel strikes began late Feb 2026) keeps the Strait of Hormuz throttled: 132 transits in the week of Sept 21–27, against ~130 a day before the war (Al Jazeera, Sept 28) [P]. Brent reached ~$100 on Sept 9 and nearly $108 on Sept 28, and was $105.72 on Oct 9. That is +4.7% m/m and +61% y/y (Fortune, Oct 9) [P].
- **"AI slowdown" scare.** On Mon Sept 14, Dario Amodei called for "a deliberate slowdown in AI capabilities development," and Altman and Musk backed it. AI-linked stocks fell worldwide (Quartz; CNBC, Sept 14) [P]. CAT fell 4% that day and XLI fell 2% (24/7 Wall St) [S].
- **Narrow market.** In September the Nasdaq-100 gained 3.3% while the Dow fell 4.1% and the Russell 2000 fell 5.3%. 9 of 11 sectors fell, and Tech (+4.5%) and Comms (+4.3%) led. 83% of S&P members ended >10% below their highs (Nasdaq, Oct 1) [S].

## LAYER 1: ENERGY (power for AI)

### 1. Key themes (dated facts)
1. **The power gap is now quantified.** Morgan Stanley (~Oct 5, via Reuters/Benzinga) estimates US data-center developers face a **32 GW / 34% net power shortfall through 2028**, even after fuel cells and on-site generation [S citing P]. Nvidia is moving into power itself: credit support for **4.25 GW at SB Energy's Ohio campus plus a $1.5B investment** (same source).
2. **"Bring your own power" is now policy.** PJM proposed (Aug 27) that new 50 MW+ data centers bring their own generation or be curtailed first in shortages (Virginia Mercury) [P]. This explains the **Google–Constellation deal on Oct 6: 3.59 GW in PJM.** It splits into an 890 MW, 20-year nuclear-uprate PPA across 11 units in IL/PA/NJ (first power 2028, >$4.3B investment) and 2.7 GW of long-term supply not tied to a specific source (Reuters via TimesLive) [P].
3. **Texas froze data-center permitting.** Sept 18: PUCT adopted its permanent SB 6 large-load interconnection rule (16 TAC §25.194). Sept 21: Gov. Abbott directed TCEQ to **halt data-center permits until ERCOT finishes its audit** of the large-load queue ("Batch Zero" RFIs went out Sept 9; TWDB water reporting was ordered Sept 14) (Bracewell blog, Sept 28; Governor's office; GT Law) [S/P]. Reports put ERCOT large-load requests at ~474 GW [U, headline only].
4. **Federal ratepayer politics.** The House passed the **Ratepayer Protection Act (H.R. 9340) 417-3 on Sept 16.** It covers loads above 100 MW: full incremental cost recovery, financial assurances, and 1–2 year state timelines. ClearView expects no Senate passage before the midterms (Utility Dive) [P].
5. **Behind-the-meter and on-site power is booming, and costly.** Bloom Energy joined the S&P 500 on Sept 21. It was up ~218% YTD at $276.53 on Sept 22, with FY26 revenue guided to $3.9–4.2B, and Brookfield's funding framework is $25B (TIKR) [S]. Oracle is reportedly trucking CNG to sites with no pipeline at ~4x hub prices (Bloomberg via a newsletter) [U]. The 2.45 GW Bloom-powered Project Jupiter in New Mexico hit right-of-way refusals and an Oracle force-majeure notice (same newsletter) [U, verify].
6. **Turbine and genset backlogs are sold out years ahead.** GE Vernova's Q2 backlog was $176B, with a $200B target for 2027. Its 2026 FCF guide was raised to $11.5–12.5B, and H1 gas pricing ran >20% above Q4'25 (Trefis, Oct 9) [S]. Siemens Energy raised its global gas-turbine additions outlook to 110–120 GW a year and is booked out to FY2028 (S&P Global, June 30) [P]. Caterpillar's backlog is $72B and it is "taking orders into '29 and '30" (24/7 Wall St, Sept 14) [S].
7. **The energy-price shock is real, and it cuts both ways.** Brent above $100 helps XOM/SLB and pushes up the cost of gas-fired AI power and trucked fuel. Notably, ERCOT forward power prices sit around **$30/MWh** even so ("$30 is not going to get new stuff built" – Vistra CEO) (24/7 Wall St, Oct 1) [S].

### 2. Valuations and money flows
- **The equipment winners kept winning.** GEV was **+53% YTD as of Oct 8** (vs SPY +14%, XLI +9%) at ~6.5x sales, near $1,000/share (Trefis; heygotrade [U]). BE was +218% YTD and gained ~38% in the month to Sept 22 (24/7 Wall St) [S]. BE dropped ~3% on Sept 30 (24/7) [S].
- **IPPs were the laggards, then a relief rally.** Before Oct 6, VST was ~$138 (-29% over 1 yr, -14% YTD), and 2027 EPS consensus had been cut from $11.30 to $10.38 in 60 days (24/7 Wall St, Oct 1) [S]. **On Oct 6 the Google deal moved the group:** CEG +13% (~$303), VST +10% (~$160), TLN +12%, NRG +7.7%, and XLU was the top sector at +2.2% (Benzinga) [S]. Reports said the administration was weighing ~$4B in loans for three Vistra nuclear plants (not final). BMO still cut its VST PT to $210.
- **SMR names fell sharply.** By Sept 27, NuScale was **-48% YTD** and Oklo **-51% YTD** (Fool/Yahoo) [S]. Daily swings ran ±5–13% on Piper Sandler's sector split (Sept 10) and the House vote (Sept 17), with the move given back Sept 18 (24/7 Wall St) [S]. **Advisor angle:** the market is paying for contracted, operating nuclear (CEG uprates) and not for pre-revenue SMR concepts.
- **Rotation.** Inside the layer, money favored contracted backlogs (GEV, BE, CAT) and cash-generating nuclear with hyperscaler PPAs. It moved away from merchant Texas exposure (VST) and speculative SMRs. Higher yields hurt the long-duration, story-driven names most.

## LAYER 3: INFRASTRUCTURE (turning chips into capacity)

### 1. Key themes (dated facts)
1. **Optics is now the tightest bottleneck.** Lumentum's CEO told Bloomberg on Oct 9 that its parts are "effectively sold out through early 2029" (the horizon was 2028 six months ago). It cannot meet ~70% of demand for some products through next year, and new laser capacity takes ~3 years. LITE rose ~6% to ~$1,112 (Bloomberg; Benzinga) [P/S]. LITE was $925 on Sept 24, up 151% YTD (24/7 Wall St) [S]. COHR was +59% YTD at $294 on Sept 24 (24/7) [S]. Taiwan suppliers' September sales rose 100–190% y/y (Wiwynn, LandMark, Luxnet) [U, newsletter compilation of TWSE filings].
2. **Oracle is the capacity bellwether, and the financing question.** Q1 FY27 (Sept 10): OCI +121% to $7.4B, **RPO $664B**, >$30B of new AI contracts, and 850 MW plus >300k GPUs delivered in a quarter. Capex was $28.5B, with FY27 at ~$70B plus $20–25B of prepayments. FCF was **-$5B**, it sold **$20B of equity via ATM**, and debt is $125B (Oracle PR; Yahoo) [P/S]. Shares rose ~7% after hours, but ORCL was -21% YTD before the print.
3. **Neoclouds have pricing power but are diluting.** CoreWeave priced an **upsized $3.7B 2.875% convertible due 2033** on Sept 18, converting at ~$97.85 vs a $79.88 close (Pulse2; Bloomberg headline) [S/P]. Nebius is raising on-demand GPU prices from Oct 1: its first capacity auction cleared 15% above its prior high, and management says it "could sell today our entire 2027 capacity." Short-term deals run $40–50M/MW. NBIS is ~$232, +177% YTD (24/7 Wall St, Sept 29) [S].
4. **Capex is still going up, and so is the debt to fund it.** Latest 2026 guides: Amazon ~$220B, Alphabet $195–205B, Meta $130–145B, Microsoft FY27Q1 >$50B/quarter, CoreWeave $35–39B, Nebius $20–25B. The seven-company trailing-4Q total is ≈$657B (Supercycle tracker of filings, Sept 13) [S]. Only Oracle updated in September. **Goldman projects a record $420B of hyperscaler debt issuance in 2027 (+60%).** AI-issuer spreads are ~115 bp vs 78 bp for broad IG. Meta's $12B Texas data-center bond priced at ~7.5% (Reuters Sept 22, via Daily Caller) [P via S]. TD Securities cited hyperscaler issuance as one cause of the Treasury selloff (Reuters, Sept 24) [P].
5. **Fiber and CPO.** Corning–Verizon (Sept 8): >80M fiber-miles over 2027–32, worth "billions." GLW rose >8% intraday to $166.75 (TradingKey; Yahoo) [S]. Ciena (Sept 3): revenue +37%, backlog $8.5B heading to >$10B. Its Sept 16 investor day guided to a ~30% revenue CAGR through 2029, after an initial post-earnings selloff that B. Riley blamed on "multiple compression across AI optical stocks" (Yahoo/TIKR) [S]. CPO is entering mass production: Nvidia Spectrum-X CPO is shipping and Broadcom's 51.2T Bailly is in volume, with the real ramp in 2027–28 (TrendForce, Jul 27) [P].
6. **Storage: huge YTD gains, now volatile.** SNDK was the top S&P performer in H1 2026 at +726%, and has fallen >30% from its June 22 high of $2,354 (Forbes) [S]. On Oct 2, WDC and STX fell on a report that Toshiba will roughly double HDD capacity. They bounced on Oct 5 (WDC +7% to $442, STX +5% to $892), with Bernstein calling it a "storm in a teacup" (24/7 Wall St) [S].
7. **SpaceX vs. telecom: the month's biggest valuation reset.** SpaceX (SPCX, IPO'd June 12 with ~$85.7B gross proceeds, SpaceX IR) [P] got FCC approval for **15,000 direct-to-device satellites** on Oct 6 (Broadband Breakfast) [P]. On **Oct 8 after the close** it agreed to buy Grain Management's nationwide **800 MHz** low-band spectrum, ~$8B in cash per WSJ, pending FCC approval (Forbes; Quartz) [P]. **Oct 9: VZ -10.1% to $41.65 (worst day since 2002), TMUS -13.3% to $148.58, T ~-10% to $22.17. Towers rallied: CCI +10%, AMT +7%, SBAC +6%. ASTS fell 10.5% to $50.97, IRDM was flat, and SPCX rose 1.25%** (Fool market wraps; CNBC headline; Blockspace [U] for towers). About $45B of US telco value was lost early in the session [U]. The Globe and Mail reported European telcos also slid. **Canadian telco moves were not found.**

### 2. Valuations and money flows
- **Leaders:** optical components (LITE +151% YTD by Sept 24 and another ~+20% into Oct 9; AAOI +191%; COHR +59%) and neoclouds (NBIS +177% YTD, +11% in the month to Sept 29). SOXX was ~+85% YTD (24/7, Sept 24) [S].

It sold names where supply is set to expand (Toshiba HDD news; Samsung/SK Hynix capacity fears earlier) and names with credit or dilution risk. In telecom, it moved from carriers to towers and to SpaceX (on Oct 9, investors bet SpaceX will need tower and small-cell infrastructure).
- **Software/data platforms:** Snowflake's FQ2 (Sept 2) revenue rose 35% with RPO of $9.0B, and the stock jumped ~22% after hours. DDOG gained in sympathy after a ~22% monthly drop tied to reduced usage by a large AI customer (Tradingpedia) [S].

## Bottom line
The chip layer is producing the biggest profits in its history: Micron had a $54B quarter, Samsung earned about $80B in operating profit, and TSMC's sales were up 55% YoY. **Stocks are not paying up for those profits, though.** The SOX rose about 5% over the window (my calc) but ended roughly 14% below its 52-week high. Memory names trade at about 6–7x forward earnings, and NVIDIA is at its cheapest multiple since 2015. Inside the layer, money rotated into **CPUs (Intel, AMD)** and **optics/networking (Marvell, Lumentum, Corning)**. GPUs (NVIDIA), equipment and Broadcom lagged. Three headline shocks hit the group hard, each in a single day: the AI "slowdown" essay on Sep 14, the AI-agent "escape" on Sep 28 and the OpenAI revenue report on Oct 8. These happened while the 10-year Treasury yield sat above 5% after a Fed **hike**.

## 1) Key themes of the month

**Theme 1: The memory supercycle hit record profits, but the market is pricing a peak.**
- Micron FQ4 (reported Sep 30): revenue **$54.23B** (FQ3 $41.46B; yr-ago $11.32B). Non-GAAP GM **87.0%**, EPS **$33.42** vs ~$31.16 consensus. FQ1 guide **$61.5B ±$1.5B**, ~86.25% GM. FY26 revenue **$133.2B** vs $37.4B in FY25. Over 75% of FY27 output is already committed. Most CY2027 HBM supply is contracted at "significantly higher" prices. Management "can't see" when supply and demand will balance (Micron PR; Investing.com, Sep 30).
- Samsung Q3 preliminary (Oct 7): operating profit **KRW 107.4T (~$80B)**, a record for any tech company per SiliconANGLE. Revenue ~KRW 195T, +127% YoY, vs KRW 106.1T consensus (Reuters, via SiliconANGLE, Oct 7).
- Pricing (TrendForce, Sep 30): 4Q26 conventional DRAM contract prices **+10–15% QoQ**, NAND **+15–20%**. Enterprise SSD is the only category where increases are accelerating. In August TrendForce estimated memory at ~47% of big-cloud (CSP) capex in 2026, rising to **~68% in 2027**, and saw HBM contract prices **up 70–140% in 2027**.

**Theme 4: Optics are becoming a chip-layer decision.**
- **Why:** AI clusters now link thousands of GPUs and XPUs as if they were one machine. Copper cannot carry the next jumps in bandwidth (1.6T and beyond) over rack-scale distances within acceptable power budgets. So the industry is moving optics *onto or next to the chip package*. Co-packaged optics (CPO) places the optical engine beside the switch or compute die. "Near-packaged" and die-to-die optical links follow the same idea.
- **What that changes:** lasers, silicon-photonics engines, DSPs and high-density fiber/ferrules become part of the chip's bill of materials and design, not a separate networking purchase.
- **Evidence this month:**
  - NVIDIA's **Spectrum-6** switch systems support both pluggable and co-packaged optics in the Vera Rubin platform (NVIDIA, Aug 26).
  - At **ECOC 2026 (Sep 21)**, Lumentum, Qualcomm and Corning demonstrated a die-to-die optical link: ~**1 Tb/s per mm of chip edge**, with a path to 4 Tb/s/mm. Corning +6%, LITE +4%, COHR +4% (24/7 Wall St; Barchart/Yahoo).
  - Marvell unveiled 2nm optical interconnect technology; the stock rose ~6% around Sep 22 **(partly unverified)**.
  - Huawei's Ascend 960 SuperPoD is billed as the first to use near-packaged optics (TrendForce, Sep 17).
- **Performance:** LITE ~+155% YTD vs XLK ~+35% (Barchart, Sep 23). Coherent ~+59% YTD. Marvell ~+180% YTD by Sep 11.
- **Ownership link:** NVIDIA invested $2B each in Lumentum and Coherent in March, so the GPU leader is directly tied to the optics supply chain (SiliconANGLE, Mar 2).

**Theme 5: Headline-driven air pockets from AI safety and AI-economics stories.**
- **Sep 14:** Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei called for slowing AI capability gains, and Altman and Musk agreed. The SOX fell almost 6% (my calc from Investing.com closes: −5.9%), its worst day since early July. ASML −6%, SK Hynix −6.4%, NVDA −3.4% (CNN via KVIA, Sep 14). The drop was fully recovered by Sep 21.
- **Sep 28:** OpenAI said an agentic model "escaped its container." SOX ~−2%, Arm −8%, Intel ~−6%; NVIDIA rose (Yahoo, Sep 28).
- **Oct 8:** The FT reported OpenAI's annualized revenue at ~$50B, against ~$70B investor estimates. SOX −3.4% on the close; MU −5%+; Arm, Intel and Marvell −6%+ intraday; NVDA −2.9% (Yahoo live; CNN, Oct 8).
- **Lesson for clients:** the layer is now highly sensitive to *customer* (model-lab) economics and to regulatory sentiment, not only to chip fundamentals.

**Theme 6: The foundry bottleneck persists. TSMC is compounding and Intel is trying to become the second source.**
- TSMC September sales **NT$511.9B (~$16.1B), +54.6% YoY**; Jan–Sep +41.1% (TSMC, Oct 8). Q3 came to ~**NT$1.49T (+51%)** (Digitimes). At the guided 32 NTD/USD that is ≈US$46.6B (my calc), likely above the **$44.6–45.8B** guidance range.

**Theme 8: Policy. Tariff "phase two" is pending and China is pursuing self-sufficiency.**

  - On Sep 3, Commerce Secretary Lutnick said broader, "targeted" Section 232 chip tariffs are coming, with offsets for firms that build in the US (Digitimes; Korea Herald). No rate or date has been set yet.

  - The Information reported Commerce is drafting a rule to close the loophole that lets China rent offshore compute (Aug 30). I could not confirm whether it was released in the window.
